    Connect PulseDeck to your apps

    Mic + sounds→PulseDeck→CABLE Input→CABLE Output→Your app
    1. Install a virtual audio cable once.

      VB-CABLE adds a playback device called CABLE Input and a microphone called CABLE Output. Run its installer as administrator and restart Windows if it asks.

    2. Choose your devices in the mixer.

      Click Scan devices, pick your real microphone, choose CABLE Input as the broadcast output, then click Connect audio.

    3. Pick CABLE Output as the microphone in each app.

      It's the one listed under recording/input devices (CABLE Input never shows up there). Keep PulseDeck running — minimizing is fine.

    If some sounds don't come through in a game or Discord

    Voice chat is built to pass speech and reject everything else. Three settings in the receiving app decide whether your clips survive:

    Noise suppression (Krisp, RNNoise, "voice isolation") strips music and effects while letting speech through — turn it off. Voice activity / input sensitivity drops anything quieter than its threshold — set it to manual and lower it, or use push-to-talk and hold the key while a clip plays. Automatic gain control pumps levels unpredictably — turn it off too. Keep Auto-level sounds on in PulseDeck so quiet clips land at a consistent, speech-like loudness, and raise the Soundboard slider into the Boost range if you still need more.

    Any app that accepts a Windows microphone can usually use the cable. A few games only read the Windows default device or reserve keyboard shortcuts; for those, set the Windows default recording device to CABLE Output and use the on-screen overlay instead of hotkeys.

    Replay buffer — grabbing what someone else said

    Turn the buffer on in the Replay buffer tab and PulseDeck keeps the last 30 s–3 min of everything that plays through your headphones (Discord, game chat, the game itself) in memory. When something happens, press Ctrl+Alt+R or the overlay's Clip button to save it, then trim it and add it to the board. It hears your default Windows output, so keep Discord and the game on that device (not the cable). Tick Start when PulseDeck opens to have it always running. It records other people, so let your friends know.

    Using the game overlay

    Press Ctrl+Alt+O (or click Overlay) for a small always-on-top deck you can click while playing. Drag it by its title bar, resize from any edge, and set its transparency at the bottom. It stays above windowed and borderless-windowed games; games in exclusive fullscreen cover every overlay, so switch the game's display mode to Borderless or Windowed fullscreen.

    Previews stay in your headphones

    The playback-region editor, Sound Studio, the recorder, and Text to speech preview only through the headphones chosen under Headphones in the mixer. They never play through the broadcast output, and if no headphones are chosen PulseDeck asks you to pick them instead of using your speakers. Speak now and pads are the only things that reach your apps.

    Other programs that record your computer's system audio, including PulseDeck's own replay buffer, can hear whatever plays through your default output. If that output is your headphones, they may also record previews. Pick a different default output while previewing if that matters.
